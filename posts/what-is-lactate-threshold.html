What Is Lactate Threshold?

October 28, 2026

Lactate threshold is the exercise intensity at which lactate begins to accumulate in the blood faster than your body can clear it. Below it, you can sustain effort for hours. Above it, fatigue is a countdown.

The physiology in one breath

During hard exercise, muscle cells produce lactate through glycolysis. At low intensities your body recycles it efficiently — as fuel for the heart, brain, and slow-twitch fibers. Push past threshold and production outruns clearance. Blood lactate climbs, pH drops, and the burning sensation follows.

Key shift in understanding: lactate is not a waste product. It's a signaling molecule and a major energy source. The threshold isn't about "going anaerobic" — it's about the crossover point where glycolytic flux exceeds oxidative clearance.

Why it predicts performance better than VO2max

Two athletes can have identical VO2max values and wildly different race results. The one who can hold a higher percentage of that max without lactate stacking wins. Threshold is the better proxy for what you can actually sustain in a race.

How it's measured

The standard method: graded exercise test with blood draws at each stage, plotted as a lactate curve. The inflection point — where the curve bends upward — is your threshold. Definitions vary (first rise, fixed 2 or 4 mmol, D-max, individual anaerobic threshold), which is why comparing studies requires checking which criterion was used.
